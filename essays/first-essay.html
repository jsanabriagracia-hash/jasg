Personal Statement

Everyone has a life changing event. For me, it was the pass of Hurricane Maria through Puerto Rico. During this difficult situation, I came to think how was I going to be remember when I die. Going through this made me realize that I want to be known as a person who is willing to work as hard as he can to fulfill his goals. My father told me once that I should try my best at being the best. Each day brings a new opportunity to being at least better than before. My childhood took place in a poor community with low opportunities to a good education; in addition, I had 6 surgeries in my legs because of a deformity I had in my bones. Hence, that didn’t stop my parents from guiding me and my sister through the right path and getting us a good education. They fought so hard for me and I want to make them feel proud. I want to honor the sacrifice of my parents and be an example to my community and others that had been through situations similar to mine by becoming a professional in something that I’m passionate about as chemistry. I want to show that being a Latino, dark skinned and gay, as myself, does not represent a limitation anymore to fulfill your aspirations. Having your mind set and focus into your goals and working as hard as you can to reach it, is enough to achieve happiness and reach your goals in life. In my undergraduate career, I took chemistry courses that strengthened my desire of furthering my career with a Ph. D in chemistry. In the University of Puerto Rico at Cayey’s campus, all chemistry major students must propose, conduct and expose an investigation as part of the Organic chemistry laboratory course and in the Capstone. For the organic chemistry research, my first exposure to research, me and my two partners compare the total amount of phenolic compounds (Compounds that are proven to cure and/or treat a variety of diseases) in the husk of cocoa obtained from Puerto Rico and Mexico. We also compare three different techniques (Soxhlet, Ultrasound and Microwaves assisted extraction) for the extraction and prove which one provides the best results. This experience helped me to appreciate the opportunity of research. When doing research, you have to be open minded toward learning from other disciplines in order to find your results or to ask yourself a new question that may get you closer to your conclusions. Every victory or mistake comes with the opportunity to learn. Having the will of learn should be the best way to approach doing research. On the other hand, the aim of my Capstone research was to compare UV-vis Spectroscopy and Atomic Absorption Spectroscopy in the quantification of iron obtained from Spinacia Oleracea (Spinach). From this experience I learned the importance of time management. This laboratory involved the preparation of samples, calibration curve, conditioning of the equipment, extraction of samples and proper cleaning of the instruments. Before arriving to the laboratory, I made habit to create a work plan. There were times in the performance of an experiment, that I just couldn’t decide what to do next, because of the big load of work that had to be done. In this work plan I distributed the tasks by time and priority. That way, the working flow didn’t get interrupt. My desire of gaining knowledge and my passion for research got me in the research groups of Professor Juan Santana (Computational Chemistry) and Ramón Hernández (Inorganic Chemistry). In the inorganic research group, we got into the chemistry of coordination complex. Synthetizing and characterizing molecules with chemotherapeutic potential. My enthusiasm for the analytical part of our research ended with me behind the characterization of our products; running the analysis with NMR-H and FTIR. It was also my duty the interpretation of the spectra and writing a report to our mentor of our findings. On the other hand, during my time making research with Juan Santana I gain experience with software capable of understanding the behavior of molecules in different chemical systems. I studied the interaction between sulfur atoms in a nickel surface FCC (111) in order to understand the anchoring mechanism of Lithium polysulfides in the cathode of Li-S batteries caused by the Li-Polysulfide shift. I worked with software like Material Studio and VESTA for modeling and with Python for the optimization of these systems. Our hard work provided me with the opportunity of attending SACNAS 2018 in San Antonio, Texas. I also presented my research in the university at a Symposium coordinated by the Chemistry Department. These research experiences provided me with knowledge far beyond of what a classroom could offer. I believe that knowledge develops greatness in people. I will never stop chasing the opportunity of learning and understanding more about myself. For me doing research is not just a way to discovered the world, but also to discovered yourself. While doing research I learned what are my strengths and weaknesses. I’ve also learned that, sometimes, thinking different is the best way to solve problems and how to work with people that doesn’t think the same way as I do. I learned that you can learn as much from your victories as form your mistakes and how to work with my weaknesses in order to achieve a specific purpose. I’ve came to realize that I want to share this experiences with others so they could understand the importance of learning like I do. That’s why I coordinated a meeting with the science teachers from my High School back in my hometown to discuss the possibility of a laboratory course. That meeting ended up with the start of a proposal. That proposal was showed to the principal of the school and in less of the expected time, it turned into a reality. I was the instructor of the laboratory which was offered every Friday. I was responsible for creating the handouts with the instructions and procedure and I also helped the students each time they had any doubts. We had the support of the Department of Education of Puerto Rico that provided supplies and reagents which we used in the course. Also, the Interamerican University of Puerto Rico at Guayama which provided laboratory equipment such as pipets, burets, etc. This was such a great experience; helping students understand new topics, guiding them through the thinking process, providing analytical skills to solve problems and earning their gratitude. I knew it wasn’t going to be my last experience. The extreme circumstances caused by Maria forced me to adapt to a new reality and change the path to fulfill my goals. My original plan involved going straight to graduate school after completing my bachelor’s degree in chemistry, however due to the effects of the hurricane I faced the need to help my family financially. I took this as a chance to do what I like and teach. I got a job at a private school being one of their science teachers. The experience was great. It helped me reinforce the fact that I want to become a professor. I want to plant the seed of curiosity and help others understand the importance of learning as it will help them become better every day. I learned from this job, the proper way to prepare myself to offer a class, I was motivated enough to do literature research to find teaching techniques and ways to get the attention of the students meanwhile being the coordinator of the science fair, the Eco Club and the robotics club. The school and the Senate of Puerto Rico were so impressed and grateful with my commitment in work that they honored me with a recognition (official signed document) from the Senate of Puerto Rico for maintaining a job of excellence even during the Covid-19. The clubs that I ran meanwhile I was a teacher, got me thinking that, when being a professor, I will not just provide professional advice to my students in a classroom. I will also have my students in my research laboratory. Hence, I wanted to gain more experience on instrument utility and software management. After almost a year being a teacher, I applied to Environmental Quality Laboratories (EQ Labs.) for the job of Laboratory Technician. It was one of my duties to assure the absence of herbicides in drinking water samples from Dasani, Nikini and the water company of Puerto Rico. The experience I’ve gained provided me with preparation enough to run by myself analysis involving laboratory equipment such as HPLC with PDA, UV and Fluorescent detector, UPLC, GCMS and GC with FID detector. I’ve also strengthened my skills in preparation of solutions, physical separation techniques and software management. EQ Labs was a place where I grew as a chemist. My passion for chemistry cannot be clearer now with this experience. That makes my path to fulfilling my goals as clear as it can get. Learning and sharing ideas with experienced professionals in the field, getting feedback and working together for a common purpose is a similar scenario of a research lab. Even though, leaving my jobs were tough, I am still focus on my goals of becoming the best professor I can be for myself and my students in the future. In essence, my appreciation for developing knowledge from every life experience build in me the desire of becoming a professor and guide others throughout their learning experience. Serving as a professor and mentor I will fulfill my passion. In order to do that I want to become the best version of me by earning a Ph. D. Every day gives you the opportunity to become a better version of yourself, and that is what I intend to do. I hope you honored me by awarding me with this fellowship as I will do whatever it takes to show my gratitude for your decision by being one of the best students while I’m in the Ph.D. program. And after, by being one of the best professors out there providing my service to the world.
