Heterobimetallic ZrCo for C-H Bond Activation

Manuscript in process

Over the years, C–H bond activation and functionalization have been predominantly carried out using second- and third-row transition metals. To improve the economic viability of these reactions, the use of first-row transition metal catalysts has become increasingly important. We use heterobimetallic complexes for the goal of performing catalysis using more Earth-abundant metals. By combining a Lewis acidic moiety (an early transition metal) with a late transition metal, we observe metal–metal cooperativity in molecular transformations (Figure 1). The polarized metal–metal bond in our systems allows redox events to occur at milder potentials, giving access to cobalt in negative oxidation states.

The use of an arene-bound Zr(IV)/Co(–I) precursor has demonstrated successful C–H activation and functionalization of nitrogen-containing substrates. (Figure 2, A & D). A nitrogen ligand aids in stabilizing the low-valent Co center but is labile enough to decrease its coordination number through a dissociation mechanism, thereby opening a coordination site for the functionalization substrate to bind (Figure 2, B). Substitution on these substrates during the C–H bond activation step influences the electronic structure at the cobalt center. Through single-crystal XRD and DFT calculations, we have proposed a mechanism and predicted the catalytic cycle (Figure 2).
